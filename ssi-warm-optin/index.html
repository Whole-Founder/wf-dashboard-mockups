Why this shape. A real accordion with the first row open, so the section costs half a fold closed and cannot become a wall of text. Rows are questions in the reader's own phrasing, not headings, because a reader scanning for their doubt is matching wording rather than reading.

Answers at 30 to 50 words, which is long enough to answer without hedging and short enough that the reader does not feel handled. The section as a whole is the smallest full section on the page at 0.45 folds, which is correct for something most readers will never open.

Cutting the fit block removed the only place the page addressed timing, which on a warm list is usually the real objection rather than suitability. The fork now absorbs some of that, but nothing states it. If the AIR data supports timing being the top objection, the cheapest fix is a third FAQ row rather than bringing the block back.

Rules that apply to every slot

  • One CTA label family. Get on a Call, identical at the header, the soft ask, the fork and the sticky mobile bar. The watch-next links are a path, not a second button, and they are styled quieter for that reason.
  • Cleared proof only. Any name, quote or number that is not cleared stays out. It is never replaced with a plausible substitute. Eight open flags are marked in amber on the page above.
  • No em dashes. House rule, applies to every line on the page.
  • Budgets are derived, not inherited. Each one comes from what its section has to do, which is why three of them moved during the copy stage. A budget you cannot write inside is a structure question: raise it, do not quietly overrun it.
  • Decisions A to D are recorded in place on the page above, including the three the copy stage revised, with the reasoning. Nothing is blocking copy except the proof-wall subhead and three FAQ rows.
How to read and use this wireframe optional

A wireframe locks structure, not words. It answers one question: is this laid out in the right order, with the right emphasis, to move this visitor to action? This version has passed through the copy stage, so the words on it are now real copy rather than placeholders, and the flags in amber are the things that still are not settled.

Three views

Full preview is the page itself: the real type at the real size, on real page spacing, scrolling without interruption. No section labels, no rails, no annotation. This is the view for judging whether the page works.

Specs layers the build detail back on: what each box is, how long it runs, and what job it has to do.

Persuasion is the new one. It opens a rail beside each section explaining why that section is there, why it takes the shape it does, and why it runs as long as it does. Switch at the top, or open one section at a time with the faint buttons on its top right.

Why the third view exists

Specs tells you a section gets 80 to 100 words. It never told you why, and a budget with no reasoning behind it reads as arbitrary. This page is the proof of that: the bridge was budgeted at half a fold because another page's bridge was half a fold, and when it was written properly it needed a full one. The budget was wrong, not the copy.

So budgets here are derived from the job, and Persuasion shows the derivation. It is the view for anyone deciding whether the page is right, as opposed to building it.

Who uses which view

  • Reviewer (Gray, the client): Full preview to judge the page, Persuasion to challenge it. Sign off on section order and emphasis before money goes to design.
  • Copywriter: Specs to write, Persuasion to argue with a budget that does not fit the job. The same content is in the copy brief below and in the .md shipped alongside.
  • Designer and developer: Specs. Layout, counts and fold budgets are the build contract.

Two conventions worth knowing

  • Tags. SWAP changes per variant, CONSTANT is written once and reused, CUSTOMIZE V2 is constant now and hand-tuned per video later.
  • Amber means blocked. Something we genuinely do not have yet: an uncleared number, a missing disclaimer, a slot nobody has written. It is never filled with a plausible substitute. There are eight on this page.

The dark bar at the top, the view switch, the section summary and this block are all wireframe scaffolding. None of it exists on the real page.
