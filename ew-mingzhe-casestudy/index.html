(On two hours a weeknight and most of his weekends, around a full‑time job)

Dear working dad,

  • Do weekend plans with your kids get pushed back for work?
  • Do your days run 8 to 10 hours, sometimes 12?
  • Have you tried something on the side before, and watched it go nowhere?

That was Ming Zhe, too.

For 15 years he has been a solution architect at the same software company in Singapore.

He likes the work.

But the days run 8 to 10 hours, sometimes 12, and some weekends too.

He has a wife and a six‑year‑old son.

About his son, in his words: “When you look at his face, he look very disappointed.”

He had tried a store once before, in 2020, on his own.

He bought stock, kept it at home and posted it on Shopee.

Nothing sold.

“I don’t know where to start. I just blindly test here, test there.”

He had also been burned by a forex program that promised quick money.

So before he trusted anyone again, he did his homework.

He watched every video Rayston Heem had shared.

Two things decided it: the student results, and that Rayston is local.

He joined Ecomwarts in February 2026, before he told his wife.

His rule was two hours every weeknight, and most of the weekend.

He used AI tools for the product research, the product images and the store design.

His first 19 products didn’t take off.

One or two orders a day.

His wife asked whether the course was really working.

Around product 15, he thought about stopping.

Then, on the coach calls, he heard students who had tested 60 and 80 products and kept going.

“So what is 15 to me, right?”

Product 20 was different.

On day five it passed $1,000 USD in sales in a day.

In the 30 days from July 11, 2026, his store did $154,126 USD in sales.

Watching other students’ interviews, he had figured it would take him two years.

It took about six months.

Where Ming Zhe is now:

  • He still has the job he likes
  • About two hours on weeknights go to the store, more on weekends
  • His best day so far is about $7,600 USD in sales
  • His wife now asks him, “What can I help?”
  • His plan is to turn the store into his own brand
