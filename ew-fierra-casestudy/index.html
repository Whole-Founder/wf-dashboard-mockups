Before the store

For 15 years, her laptop came on every family holiday

Fierra was a project manager in Singapore. She worked about 40 hours a week, and her overtime went unpaid.

Her mornings started with Microsoft Teams, then email. When the family went away, the projects kept running, so the laptop came too. She took customer calls from overseas.

“I was physically present with my family. But mentally, I'm not there. I'm absent.”Fierra, on her holidays as a project manager

Then, at 35, she was laid off. She had two children, aged seven and two, and a small payout from the company to carry her through the next few months.

“I have liabilities, I have responsibilities. So I can't waste time.”Fierra, on the weeks after the layoff

She saw two paths. Find another job, or build the business she had always wanted. She chose to test the business and gave herself one to three months. If it failed, she would go back to a full-time job.

She even took a job as a safety net while she built. She left it after two weeks, because the store's results were already coming in. Her husband was the family's only earner while she built it, and he backed her fully.

The decision

She had tried dropshipping before.This time she got a mentor.

Six or seven years earlier, Fierra had tried dropshipping on her own. She knew the model was real. What she did not have now was time to relearn it from scattered YouTube videos and Reddit threads.

So she looked for someone to learn from. She found Rayston Heem, founder of Ecomwarts, through his YouTube tutorials and the case studies he shares of his students.

“Why can't I be one of them?”Fierra, on seeing other Ecomwarts students' results

She enrolled in his program. She paused for the first couple of weeks, then committed fully.

What it took

Full days at the start, fewer hours once she knew the work

The numbers did not come from a few spare minutes. Here is how her time went, in her words.

  • At the startFull days. “At the start, it was really like a full day thing for me.”
  • First 2 to 4 weeksGetting comfortable with Shopify and Facebook ads again. She had used Shopify before, but “over the years it has completely changed.”
  • By July 2026About one to two hours a day, plus one or two deep-focus days a week of five to eight hours.
“You just have to follow the course to a T and of course ask the mentors as you go along with any questions.”Fierra, on what made the difference

What changed

Her afternoons now start at the school gate

Her mornings go to the store, and to herself. In the afternoon she picks her children up from school and spends the rest of the day with them.

On a recent family trip to China, she didn't need to check her phone for work chats or emails.

“Basically for me, it's just 100% being present in my family.”Fierra, on the trip

Asked what the store gave back, she named two things: presence with her family, and financial freedom.
