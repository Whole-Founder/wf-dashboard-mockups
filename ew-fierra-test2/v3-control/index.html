How Fierra went from 8‑hour days and unpaid overtime to $318,000 USD in store sales

Dear working mum,

  • Is Microsoft Teams the first thing you check every morning?
  • Do family holidays still come with a laptop and work calls?
  • Are you there with your family, but your mind is still at work?

That was Fierra, too.

For 15 years she was a project manager in Singapore.

About 40 hours a week, and the overtime went unpaid.

On holidays she still took calls with customers.

In her words: “I was physically present with my family. But mentally, I’m not there.”

Then, at 35, she was laid off.

She had two children, aged seven and two, and a small payout from the company to carry her for a few months.

“I have liabilities, I have responsibilities. So I can’t waste time.”

She saw two paths: find another job, or build the business she had always wanted.

She gave herself one to three months to test the business.

If it failed, she would go back to a full‑time job.

She had tried dropshipping six or seven years earlier, so she knew the model was real.

This time she didn’t want to piece it together from random YouTube videos and Reddit threads.

She found Rayston Heem through his YouTube tutorials and the case studies he shares of his students, and joined his Ecomwarts program.

She even took a job as a safety net.

She left it after two weeks, because the store’s sales were already coming in.

Her husband was the family’s only earner while she built it, and he backed her fully.

What Fierra’s days look like now:

  • Her mornings go to the store, and to herself
  • She picks her kids up from school, and the afternoon is theirs
  • About one to two hours a day on the store,plus one or two deep‑focus days a week
  • A family trip to China without checking her phone for work
  • Most of the money goes back into the store, and she pays herself a monthly salary
