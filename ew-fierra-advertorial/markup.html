“While I was physically present with my family, mentally I was absent”: why a laid-off Singapore mum of two tested an online store with a mentor

Fierra, 35, gave an online store a 3-month test with a mentor and kept job hunting as her fallback. Learn how she became a full-time mompreneur who works 2 to 3 hours a day, earns more than she did at her job, and spends more time with her kids.

By Ecomwarts·October 22, 2026·9 min read

Fierra's workday started before her kids were properly awake.

Teams and email were open first thing. The day's problems were already starting for her.

For 15 years as a project manager in Singapore, that was normal. So was packing the laptop for family holidays.

“Even on holidays, I had to bring my laptop to attend issue resolution calls with customers.” Her kids had their mum at the table, but her attention was with the client.

Then, after 15 years, she got laid off

She worked about 40 hours a week, and the extra hours went unpaid. Then, at 35, out of the blue, she was let go.

She had two young kids, “liabilities and responsibilities”, and a small severance that would only cover the basics for a few months.

“It was scary. I had a small severance payout... so time was of the essence.”

She had two options. One: find another job and repeat the cycle. Two: learn a new skill and build the business she had always dreamed of.

She wasn't starting from zero. Fifteen years as a corporate project manager had made her at home on a computer, and those skills carried over to running an online store almost right away. She had also tried dropshipping six or seven years earlier. It hadn't worked for her then, but she knew the model was real.

So she set a time limit: “I gave myself a timeframe of 1 to 3 months to test this business model...”

She kept job hunting as her fallback. Her husband backed the plan, even before the store had made anything.

What she tested: a dropshipping store built with AI, with a mentor checking her work

Dropshipping works like this: you pick a product, build an online store and run the ads that bring in customers. A supplier holds the stock and ships each order for you.

The beautiful part: there's no shop to rent and no boxes in the house. You can run the whole store from home, around school hours.

The one thing Fierra refused to do was try to build it all by herself. She had no time to waste.

She knew she wanted to work with a mentor.

“...instead of wasting time searching through random YouTube videos or Reddit threads.”

She found Rayston Heem, founder of Ecomwarts, through his YouTube tutorials and his students' stories.

“Seeing testimonials from other successful students made me ask why I couldn't achieve the same results,” Fierra said.

AI tools can now help you build everything far faster than you could before. They don't make the decisions for you. She still had to learn how to make the business work and make the calls herself.

Even having tried it before, it took her a few weeks to get comfortable with Shopify and Facebook Ads again. The first weeks took many hours, but it got easier as she gained experience.

Fierra names the sacrifice: “Dedicating full focus days to building the store was key.” Her first product got sales moving. It went well enough that she has since added a second.

The result, four months later

*Thumbnail from Fierra's July 2026 interview on Rayston Heem's YouTube channel.

Fierra reported $318,000 USD in revenue between mid-March and July 8, 2026.

That's revenue, not profit. But the point is this: Fierra didn't just avoid going back to a job. She now makes more for her family than she did after 15 years of climbing the corporate ladder.

What she values more: today her mornings go to the business, and to herself. Her afternoons start at the school gate. The rest of the day is with her kids.

In June 2026, the family went to China. “I did not need to check my phone for work chats or emails, allowing me to be 100% present with my family.”

It worked for Fierra. But is dropshipping right for you?

Fierra had doubts too: “I thought it looked unrealistic at first as well.” What settled it wasn't the attempt she had made years before. It was seeing how the model works before committing to anything.

Dropshipping may suit you if:

✓
You have a job and very little spare time — Fierra planned her test with a time limit and a fallback.
✓
You want to build something for your family — Her reason was to be present for her kids.
✓
You have a budget for ads — Running a store means paying for ads while you test. Without that budget, it won't suit you yet.
✓
You're ready to do the work — Even with AI tools, it took her weeks of relearning and full-focus days. If you're hoping for results without the work, it won't suit you.
